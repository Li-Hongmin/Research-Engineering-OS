<p class="manga-story-content"><strong>Xiaoyan begins to sort out the baseline setup.</strong></p>

<p class="manga-story-content">She took out the baseline code the teacher had given her. She looked closely at the version number: TensorFlow 1.x, written last year.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">And her code uses the latest TensorFlow 2.x.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">She checked the update log. The new version made major improvements in GPU optimization, significantly boosting compute utilization. This means the baseline’s speed and accuracy may have had inherent limitations to begin with.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content"><em>💭 So... what exactly am I proving? That my approach is better, or just that I used a newer library?</em></p>

<p class="manga-story-content">She pulled out her experiment log again. The baseline training used a CPU cluster; her method used the latest GPUs. One was run a month ago, and the other was run today. All the conditions were different.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">"Th-This... there’s no comparison."</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">She opened the email, wanting to write her advisor a detailed reply. But the words fell apart in her mind.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content"><em>💭 The advisor is right. I didn’t make a fair comparison at all. If I publish these results, people will ask me: are your improvements actually from the algorithm, or from the hardware and library upgrades? I... can’t answer.</em></p>
