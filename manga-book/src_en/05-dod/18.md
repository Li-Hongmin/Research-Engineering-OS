<p class="manga-story-content"><strong>Practice: Check at the End of Each Experiment</strong></p>

<p class="manga-story-content">From then on, Rina changed her habits. Every time she finished an experiment, she would open a checklist—just like her advisor—and ask herself:</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">✓ Can I reproduce this result again within five minutes? (Check: random seed, data version, hardware environment)</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">✓ Have I conducted ablation experiments and understood the contribution of each component?</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">✓ Have I made a fair comparison with the standard baseline?</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">✓ Is my code clear enough that someone else can understand it within 15 minutes?</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">✓ Does my commit log tell a complete story?</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">No longer “just submit it as long as the numbers look good.” No longer simply equating “done” with “the code runs.”</p>

<p class="manga-story-content"><em>💭 So it turns out the true definition of “finished” is this: you’re willing to take responsibility for every decision in this experiment in front of your advisor, reviewers, or any reader.</em></p>
