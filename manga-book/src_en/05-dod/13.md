<div class="manga-story-label">DoD Item 1 - Results Are Reproducible</div>
<p class="manga-story-content">At the thesis discussion meeting, the advisor summarized Xiao Yan’s experience into a checklist.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">"First," the advisor pointed to the first line on the whiteboard, "your results must be reproducible on another machine. Using the same code, the same data, and the same random seed."</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">Rina nodded—this was exactly the minefield she had just stepped into. She still remembered that moment, the despair when the result changed from +0.89 to +0.71.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">"Why is it important?" the mentor turned to the other students. "Because if the results can’t be reproduced, then we can’t prove whether this improvement is real or just an illusion. The foundation of science is verifiability."</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">He paused for a moment, looked at Rina, and said, "You understand now, right?"</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">Rina nodded vigorously.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content"><em>💭 I never want to go through that helpless feeling of “same code, different results” again. From now on, after every experiment, I’m going to record the entire environment, parameters, and random seeds. So that anyone can reproduce my results.</em></p>

<p class="manga-story-content">"So," the mentor continued, "this means you need to record very carefully:</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">- Code version number<br/>
- Versions of all dependencies<br/>
- Full dataset path and version<br/>
- All hyperparameters<br/>
- Random seed<br/>
- Hardware configuration"</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">"Only then, when someone asks 'Can you reproduce it?', can you confidently say 'Yes'."</p>
