<div class="manga-story-label">DoD Item 3 - Fair Comparison with the Baseline</div>
<p class="manga-story-content">"The third item is the easiest to overlook," the advisor's tone became especially serious, "your baseline must be compared with your method under exactly the same conditions. The same hardware, the same library versions, the same dataset, the same training time."</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">Xiaoyan recalled her advisor’s initial doubt: “Did you make a fair comparison with the baseline?” At the time, she didn’t understand. Now, she did.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">The instructor looked at the whole class. “If you make the baseline use an old library, old hardware, and different hyperparameters, and then claim your method is better, you’re actually cheating.”</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">He paused for a moment. “You’re not proving that your method is superior—you’re confounding variables. That’s even more dangerous than outright fabricating data, because you’ll genuinely believe your own results.”</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">Xiaoyan wrote this item down in the notebook, pressing especially hard with the pen.</p>

<p class="manga-story-content"><em>💭 The advisor is right. This is the baseline of scientific honesty. I need to remember: hyperparameters, hardware, library versions, and data versions can all affect results. If I can’t clearly explain why my comparison is fair, then all the numbers are meaningless.</em></p>

<p class="manga-story-content">The mentor continued, "So, you need to:</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">- Use a publicly available baseline implementation, or re-implement the baseline using the same library<br/>
- Train on the same dataset<br/>
- Run on the same hardware<br/>
- Use the same evaluation metrics<br/>
- Report confidence intervals or results from multiple runs<br/>
- Clearly specify all configuration details"</p>

<p class="manga-story-content">"Only in this way, when others see your results, can they confidently say: this improvement truly comes from your method, not from other factors."</p>
